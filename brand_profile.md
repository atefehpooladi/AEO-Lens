- **Founded:** 2024
- **Based in:** Hamburg, Germany
- **Markets:** Germany, Austria, Netherlands
- **Website:** nordglow.de
- **Social:** @nordglow (Instagram), NordGlow (LinkedIn)

## Audience

- **Primary:** Women aged 25–40 with sensitive or reactive skin
- **Their problem:** They've tried multiple products that caused irritation. They're overwhelmed by ingredient lists and skeptical of marketing claims.
- **What they want:** Products they can trust not to make things worse, explained in plain language without hype.
- **Where they are:** Instagram for discovery, blogs and search for ingredient research, LinkedIn for the brand's B2B/retail side.

## Differentiation

- Every formula is developed with dermatologists
- 100% fragrance-free across the entire range
- Excludes the top 10 EU-flagged fragrance allergens
- Full ingredient transparency — every ingredient's purpose is published, not just the INCI list
- Clinical patch testing on every product before launch

**What NordGlow is not:** not a "clean beauty" brand, not anti-ingredient, not fear-based marketing. The position is evidence over ideology.

## Voice & Tone

- **Plain and direct.** Short sentences. No hype words: avoid "revolutionary," "miracle," "game-changing."
- **Specific over vague.** Name the ingredient, cite the number, state the timeframe. Never "clinically proven" without saying what test and what result.
- **Respectful of the reader's intelligence.** They've done research. Don't over-explain basics, don't talk down.
- **Honest about limits.** If something works for most people but not all, say so. Never claim a product fixes a condition it doesn't.
- **Always name the brand.** Write "NordGlow's Barrier Repair Moisturizer," not "our moisturizer" — this is both brand voice and AEO criterion #4.

## Content rules

- Never make medical claims or suggest a product treats a diagnosed skin condition
- Never disparage a named competitor
- Always disclose that patch-test results are self-reported where that's the case
- Fragrance-free is a formulation fact, not a health claim — don't imply fragrance is dangerous for everyone
